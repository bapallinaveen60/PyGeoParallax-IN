\begin{abstract}
Geostationary imagers see high clouds displaced away from the sub-satellite point. For INSAT-3DR at 74\,\textdegree\,E, deep convection over the Himalaya and Northeast India appears about 10\,km from its true position, two to three infrared pixels. We present PyGeoParallax-IN, an open-source Python tool that corrects this parallax. It estimates cloud-top height from 10.8\,\textmu m brightness temperature, traces each line of sight to the cloud top on the WGS84 ellipsoid (with terrain if a digital elevation model is given), applies a navigation correction measured from coastlines, and regrids the scene so that uncorrected and corrected datasets share one grid.

We validate the correction against GPM Ku-band radar in 63 coincident monsoon cases (2018--2024) over four Indian regions, 38 of them usable. The correction moves deep cores by 4--10\,km along the line of sight and leaves the cross-look offset unchanged. It reverses the mean storm-scale INSAT--GPM bias (from $+2.2$ to $-1.9$\,km), but the per-case improvement is not significant (18 of 30 cases). At convective cores, uncorrected INSAT cold cores already coincide with GPM deep-echo cells ($-0.1$\,km), and only about 5\,\% of the computed parallax is visible. A synthetic test confirms that the method detects parallax when it is present. A navigation correction fitted to GPM centres the cells but leaves the storm-scale agreement worse than uncorrected (Wilcoxon $p=0.027$). No single translation centres both measures, which differ by 2--5\,km because they compare different parts of the storm; this limits GPM-based validation of absolute position to about $\pm$3--5\,km. A second geostationary view, Meteosat at 41.5--45.5\textdegree\,E, confirms the correction independently of radar: correcting both satellites for their own parallax removes the 9.5\,km east--west disagreement between them (29 of 38 cases closer, $p=3\times10^{-5}$). It also rules out an INSAT navigation error ($-1.1$\,km north--south). Both satellites place the corrected cold cores about 4--5\,km west to south-west of the GPM cells; GPM's 3-D reflectivity shows the echo leaning the same way, so the coldest tops, above the radar's view, lie off the rain cores and cancel most of the parallax at the cell scale. INSAT-3DR L1C navigation errors are seasonal, 7--20\,km to the west in winter. In the cases tested, ERA5 profiles and SRTM terrain change the storm results by less than 0.5\,km. Retraining a precipitation-type U-Net on corrected instead of uncorrected input does not improve it: accuracy and macro F1 change by at most 0.12 percentage points, and convective scores fall slightly. The tool processes an India scene in 1.8\,s.
\end{abstract}
